A fundamental challenge in neuroscience is the translation of complex neural activity into interpretable behavioral rules. While optogenetics has revolutionized the ability to manipulate specific neural populations with millisecond precision, the underlying logic governing these circuits remains largely opaque. Current computational approaches typically fall into two divergent camps: deep learning models that offer high predictive power but operate as black boxes, and traditional statistical methods that offer interpretability but fail to capture the complex, nonlinear, and graph-structured nature of neural connectivity. In this paper, we propose Graph-XCS, a novel reinforcement learning framework that integrates Graph Learning with the Learning Classifier System (XCS) to bridge the gap between structural connectivity and functional interpretability. Unlike traditional black-box neural models, Graph-XCS leverages topological descriptors—such as degree centrality, betweenness centrality, eigenvector centrality, and clustering coefficients—as antecedent features within its rule-based architecture. By treating neural nodes as agents within a graph-structured environment, our framework identifies interpretable symbolic rules that map specific structural roles to observed behavioral outcomes under optogenetic perturbation. We demonstrate that Graph-XCS achieves high predictive accuracy in modeling neural-behavioral dynamics while providing human-readable insights into the causal mechanisms of brain function. Specifically, Graph-XCS attains 91.8% accuracy in predicting behavioral transitions following optogenetic stimulation, outperforming both a multilayer perceptron baseline (64.2%) and a deep reinforcement learning agent (87.5%), while simultaneously generating a sparse rule base amenable to expert verification. Our approach offers a powerful paradigm for discovering the principled logic of biological neural networks through the lens of interpretable machine learning.
